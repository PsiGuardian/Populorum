# DÉMOS KRATOS
### Δῆμος Κράτος — Power belongs to the people

**FOUNDING CHARTER — Version 1.1 — 29 October 2026**

*“Let no one be condemned for thinking differently. Let each person simply be invited to explain their path, so that everyone may learn from the consequences of their own decisions.”*

*An original motto of Démos Kratos, in a Stoic spirit inspired by Marcus Aurelius as portrayed in the film Gladiator.*

---

## PREAMBLE — THE POWER TO UNDERSTAND

We, free citizens, recognise that no single individual possesses all the knowledge required to determine what is best for the people as a whole.

Every human being develops through their history, experience, observations and the consequences of their own choices.

Error is part of learning. Difference is part of freedom. Reasoning belongs to each individual.

We believe that society does not necessarily progress when one opinion defeats another, but when everyone is enabled to understand the consequences of their decisions and freely improve their own path.

We establish Démos Kratos upon one conviction:

**The people need not think alike to build a shared future.**

Démos Kratos is an open, transparent, experimental system of participatory democracy founded upon collective experience.

Its purpose is to enable the people to propose, experiment, observe, understand and revise the decisions that shape their future.

No one owns it philosophically. No one can claim to hold its sole truth.

Its existence rests on the citizens who freely choose to participate.

---

## TITLE I — INDIVIDUAL FREEDOM

### Article 1 — Equality of citizens

All citizens participate under the same rules, regardless of their origin, beliefs, resources, positions, knowledge or opinions.

No vote carries more weight on account of its author's authority, wealth or popularity.

### Article 2 — Sovereignty of reasoning

Each citizen alone governs their intellectual path.

They have the right to form, retain, change or abandon an opinion without adopting another person's view.

No one may be compelled to accept a collective line of reasoning.

### Article 3 — Freedom from judgement

No citizen may be condemned, devalued, ranked or sanctioned by Démos Kratos for an opinion, a line of reasoning or a political choice.

The difference between two paths is neither a fault nor a personal conflict.

Judgement of a person must never replace observation of facts.

### Article 4 — Coexistence of paths

When citizens encounter reasoning different from their own, they do not seek to contradict its author.

They may develop and explain their own path independently.

Each path presents its causes, hypotheses, solutions and consequences.

The platform therefore favours the coexistence of independent proposals rather than personal confrontations over opinions.

**“Do not impose your path. Illuminate your own, and let the consequences teach everyone.”**

---

## TITLE II — EMERGENT CONVERGENCE

### Article 5 — The principle of emergent convergence

Emergent convergence is the central methodological principle of Démos Kratos.

It means the gradual, voluntary coming together of individual paths through repeated observation of their consequences.

Convergence cannot be imposed. It entails neither a duty to agree nor a guarantee of unanimity.

Different paths may remain legitimate where human, social or territorial circumstances require it.

### Article 6 — The right to make mistakes

Mistakes are a permanent possibility of human experience.

They do not justify devaluing their authors.

Every citizen may reconsider a decision, revise its causes and expectations, or choose another solution.

Earlier versions of their reasoning may contribute to collective memory, subject to rules governing their personal data.

### Article 7 — Memory of experience

Démos Kratos maintains a structured record of collective experience:

- Problems identified.
- Causes put forward.
- Solutions proposed.
- Consequences anticipated.
- Decisions adopted.
- Consequences actually observed.
- Successive amendments.
- Lessons learned from the results.

This record shall enable future generations to understand earlier experiences without requiring them to adopt earlier conclusions.

---

## TITLE III — PROPOSALS AND CAUSES

### Article 8 — Right to propose

Any citizen with participation rights may submit a proposal concerning their municipality or another matter within Démos Kratos's experimental scope.

No permission may be required on the basis of education, occupation, reputation or political affiliation.

### Article 9 — The cause-and-consequence tree

Each proposal must be capable of representation as a reasoning tree containing:

1. **The problem** — What situation should be addressed?
2. **The direct cause** — What event or mechanism immediately produces it?
3. **Indirect causes** — What factors contribute to its emergence?
4. **Probable causes** — Which explanations remain hypothetical?
5. **Observed causes** — What has actually been established?
6. **The proposed solution** — What action should be tested?
7. **Likely beneficial consequences** — What benefits are expected?
8. **Likely harmful consequences** — What risks are envisaged?
9. **Observable consequences** — What indicators will verify the results?
10. **Unforeseen consequences** — What unexpected effects have arisen?
11. **Alternative solutions** — What other paths could address the problem?
12. **Revision** — What would change in light of experience?

Factual claims should be supported by sources where possible; hypotheses must be identified as such.

### Article 10 — Universal comprehensibility

The language used by Démos Kratos must be accessible to as many people as possible.

Every proposal must include an explanation in plain French, which may be supplemented by familiar, everyday, regional or differently adapted wording.

Technical, legal, scientific and administrative terminology must be explained.

In particular, each proposal must provide a summary:

**CAUSE → CONSEQUENCE → SOLUTION → JUSTIFICATION**

No citizen should be excluded from understanding a proposal by artificially complex language.

---

## TITLE IV — COLLECTIVE DECISION-MAKING

### Article 11 — Right to vote

Each eligible citizen may explain their reasoning and individually choose FOR, AGAINST or ABSTAIN. For adoption, however, each declared eligible family contributes at most one vote, regardless of its size.

The family choice follows a strict majority of its participating members’ FOR and AGAINST positions. A tie, or abstentions only, produces one family abstention. A family with no participant is non-participating. Adoption quorum and majority are calculated across families.

Family membership, aggregation, eligibility, schedule and decision criteria are published and frozen before opening. A citizen may belong to only one family in the same consultation. A position without an eligible family remains visible but has no adoption weight. Extra accounts or devices confer no extra votes.

This experimental rule applies to new consultations in this edition; historical consultations retain their frozen rules. Fictional groups are declared, never inferred from names, genealogy or sensitive data.

### Article 12 — Transparency of participation

For its experimental consultations, Démos Kratos adopts the principle of voluntary, publicly identified participation.

Before voting, participants are told that their public identity, choice and reasons may be accessible to others.

Such publication requires appropriate legal safeguards, notably concerning consent and personal-data protection.

Choosing not to participate by name does not remove anyone's right to read public proposals and results.

Démos Kratos consultations are not official elections and do not override statutory secret-ballot requirements.

### Article 13 — Territorial autonomy

Each municipality constitutes an autonomous experimental territory.

A proposal examined in one municipality has no automatic effect in another.

Each municipality may maintain its own proposals, results, assessments and experimental periods.

A measure may suit one area but not another.

The ability to reject or adapt a measure is essential to local autonomy.

### Article 14 — Sharing experience

Documented results from an experiment become available to other municipalities.

Each territory may then develop its own path based on those lessons.

No measure becomes automatically applicable in every municipality merely because a majority elsewhere adopted it.

Adoption of a real measure remains subject to the powers and procedures established by law.

### Article 15 — Symbolic collective presidency

Within Démos Kratos, the presidency is symbolised by collective decision-making, not by a permanent individual.

No citizen may claim greater decision-making power merely because they administer, develop or finance the system.

Collective decisions must be traceable and respect fundamental rights, participation rules and the legal powers of the institutions concerned.

---

## TITLE V — TRANSPARENCY AND INTEGRITY

### Article 16 — Right to verify

Every citizen may consult public information needed to understand how Démos Kratos operates, including:

- Source code.
- Consultation rules.
- Results and calculation methods.
- Versions of proposals.
- Experimental indicators.
- Project expenditure and resources.
- Technical changes affecting results.
- Administrative decisions concerning the system.

Transparency must enable verification without compromising infrastructure security or disclosing personal information that cannot lawfully be made public.

### Article 17 — Data integrity

Each recorded decision must be verifiable through cryptographic methods and auditable event logs.

Changes must be traceable.

Legitimate deletions or corrections must leave evidence of the operation where lawful, without publicly retaining data that must legally be erased.

An anomaly must trigger an investigation, not a presumption of guilt.

No citizen may be sanctioned solely on the basis of an unconfirmed technical discrepancy.

### Article 18 — Shared infrastructure

Démos Kratos code is intended to be free and open source.

Citizens may voluntarily contribute to its development, auditing, documentation and hosting.

The network may use distributed relays, verifiable copies and synchronisation mechanisms to reduce dependence on a central operator.

The sharing of computing or internet resources must always be freely, explicitly and revocably authorised.

Actual operating costs must be made public, including costs covered through volunteer work or donations.

---

## TITLE VI — PEACEFUL COEXISTENCE

### Article 19 — Separating people from their paths

Démos Kratos creates no ranking of human worth.

Paths may be consulted and their consequences studied, but opinions may not be used to establish automated political reputations or rankings of intellectual superiority.

No citizen is defined by a past decision.

### Article 20 — Protecting freedom of thought and choice

The system distinguishes freedom of opinion from conduct that violates others' rights.

Threats, identity theft, harassment, deliberate data falsification and unauthorised technical intrusion are not protected by freedom to follow one's path.

Protective measures must be proportionate, reasoned, verifiable and appealable.

They may not serve to impose a doctrine or punish political disagreement.

### Article 21 — Sharing rather than persuading

Démos Kratos does not seek to force any citizen to admit that someone else was right.

Its purpose is to make experiences and consequences available so that everyone may freely reconsider their own path.

Changes of position should arise from understanding, not submission.

---

## TITLE VII — LONG-TERM CONTINUITY

### Article 22 — Independence

Démos Kratos may not belong exclusively to a political party, business, institution or person.

Its governance rules, financing and amendments must be public and auditable.

No single contributor may unilaterally change the history of collective decisions.

### Article 23 — Amendment of the charter

This charter may evolve through a public, documented process open to citizen contributions.

Each amendment records its rationale and anticipated consequences.

No amendment may abolish fundamental rights, freedom of thought, non-discrimination or applicable legal safeguards.

### Article 24 — Enduring purpose

Démos Kratos does not aim to produce a society free of mistakes.

It seeks to build a society able to acknowledge its experiences, understand their consequences and freely derive new paths from them.

Diversity of reasoning is a resource.

Memory of consequences is a lesson.

Emergent convergence is a collective possibility.

---

## FINAL DECLARATION

We do not establish Démos Kratos to declare who is right and who is wrong.

We establish it so that every citizen can illuminate their path and future generations can learn from the experiences of those who came before them.

We do not require people to think alike.

We wish them to live together, learn from experience and make informed decisions.

**Let power belong not to anyone who claims to know for all, but to everyone willing to learn.**

**DÉMOS KRATOS**

*Power belongs to the people.*

*“Do not impose your path. Illuminate your own, and let the consequences teach everyone.”*
